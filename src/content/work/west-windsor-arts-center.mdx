## The building nobody noticed.

After eight years of planning, West Windsor township finally leased the old firehouse to
the local arts council. The building sat on a quiet street that commuters passed on their
way to the train station without a glance. The council had no marketing budget, an
audience that skewed older, and a name few residents recognized. Without a home of its
own, its events had been scattered across the library and local parks, and attendance
showed it. Eight months of renovation stood between the lease and the launch. The brief
was to make the town care before the doors opened.

## My role

As marketing committee chair, a volunteer role, I led the brand and launch end to end,
from research and strategy through the identity system, the website design and build,
email and social, print, and publicity, working with a volunteer team and donated
services.

## The brand

A firehouse turned arts center needed an identity that honored both. The approved mark
abstracts the building itself, with stepped blocks tracing the 1931 firehouse's gable,
and the tagline nods to the neighborhood. The junction where arts and community meet.
The mark had to feel vibrant enough to promise excitement and legible enough for an
audience that skewed senior. That requirement drove the palette, the type choices, and
every application from window signage to email templates.

## The building becomes the billboard

The township said no to our best idea, murals on the boarded storefronts nearby. So we
made the building advertise itself. As renovation progressed, the firehouse's own windows
became the teaser campaign, with vibrant graphics by Jeff Tryon announcing what was
coming to everyone walking to the train. The denial turned out to be a gift. The campaign
lived rent-free on the one surface we fully controlled.

## Launch on a shoestring

Everything ran on donated, free, or discounted resources. While I designed the full site,
an interim page I built in Dreamweaver kept early visitors engaged with updates. The
final site's architecture stayed deliberately simple, clean but visually stimulating and
navigable by the widest possible audience. A free Constant Contact account carried a
branded email template to 1,000 subscribers. A new Facebook page gave the center its
first social presence. A local printer ran our flyers at a discount, and two weeks before
launch, posters went up at the train station and the mall, exactly where the audience
already was.

## Results and the long run

The launch worked, well enough that The New York Times came to see it.

Sixteen years on, the center is a fixture of the greater West Windsor community, with
classes and camps, performances in theater, music and dance, literary events,
exhibitions, and community outreach.
