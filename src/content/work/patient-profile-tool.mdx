## A white paper is not a conversation.

The brief was to turn a white-paper sales aid into an Interactive Visual Aid that lets a
hematology consultant choose a patient and follow the regimen that fits, a choose your own
adventure across four hypothetical patients and the three approved POMALYST combinations.
It also had to be the proof that the new [Future State IVA](/work/interactive-visual-aids)
guidelines worked in practice, built lean on the global template rather than as another
bespoke deck. The first draft sitemap made the risk plain. It repeated the full treatment
section under every one of the four patients, four copies of the same slides, each one
adding development time, load time, and medical-legal review.

## My role

Product design, UX and UI, user testing, and storyboarding, on the first build to apply the
Future State guidelines from sitemap to sales floor. I redesigned the architecture,
tailored the content to the global templates, wrote the brand style guide for the build,
and ran QA with the in-house team through every round of iteration.

## Three presentations that feel like one

I split the deck into three. The main presentation holds Home, the four patient selector,
the individual profiles, dosing, and references. The Pd regimen became a separate hidden
presentation that the home slide links into, and the two combination regimens became a
third. Links between the three are seamless, so on the surface the rep sees one document,
while underneath every slide exists exactly once.

## The guidelines, applied

The original concept had been drawn as a generic tablet layout. I redrew it on the Future
State template, keeping the global navigation, the docked safety information, and the side
rail the system already defined, so the content fit the template rather than the template
bending to the content. A brand style guide fixed the palette, type, buttons, callouts, and
icons before development began, and gave each regimen its own color so a rep always knows
which therapy a slide belongs to.

## Compliance as navigation

Medical-legal required that a rep share the Pd data before anything else, so swiping from
the home slide leads into the Pd section with the three global navigation buttons disabled,
and the only way through is forward. Reached later from the treatment options slide, the
same section shows a new Profile button, added to the global navigation so the rep can
return to the patient they started with and switch to another. The in-house QA team and I
tested every iteration, and the storyboard carried each of these behaviors to the client
and to the field as a rehearsal script.

## Results

Reconfiguring the plan cut the timeline and the budget below the original estimate and
simplified the back-end integration. The field reps came back positive. And the build did
what it was commissioned to prove. A brand could take the Future State guidelines, from
template to style guide to storyboard, and ship a complex, regulated story as one lean
product without inventing anything new.
