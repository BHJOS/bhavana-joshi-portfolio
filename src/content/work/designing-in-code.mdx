## The static mockup stopped being enough.

By the time my Figma library had grown to several hundred high-fidelity
explorations, the questions the team was asking had outgrown what a picture of
a screen can say. Does the capture flow still feel fast on the third log of the
day? Does a gauge animation read at a glance or distract? What does a new user
see when every surface is empty? In early 2026 I moved my practice into code,
describing intent to Claude Code, reviewing the running interface, and refining
until the behavior is right. The prototype, not the mockup, became what the
team gathers around.

## My role

I am the Senior User Experience Designer on the flagship app of a health and
lifestyle nonprofit, owning the Nutrition, Health, and Wellbeing surfaces on a
12-person product team. My remit runs from research through high-fidelity UI to
a working React and TypeScript prototype the team validates real behavior in. I
started in Figma and carried the strongest directions into code.

## I run design the way engineers run code.

Designing in code only earns trust if it is disciplined. Every exploration lives
in its own worktree. Finished work goes up as a pull request the same day and
passes design, quality, and development gates before reaching the main
prototype. I also wrote Claude Code skill files for my core components, short
documents encoding each one's anatomy, states, and rules.

## One button for seven kinds of life data

Each kind of data used to have its own flow, which meant seven behaviors to
learn and seven places for friction to hide. I unified them into one capture
experience that opens from a single button anywhere in the app, with a shared
grammar of one-tap chips, in-place search, a staging tray, and compact per-type
editors. Before the build I measured the four proposed directions against the
governing spec and documented twenty gaps. That review set the build order.

## Designing for the wrong guess

AI features had to earn their place by removing effort, not adding novelty. The
clearest win, the meal photo scan, identifies the foods on a plate, prefills the
entry, and asks for a confirmation instead of typing. I designed and built it in
the prototype, and it was adopted into the team's main product surface. The real
design work was the moment the AI is wrong. Every inference shows in plain
language, and correcting it is one tap.

## Glanceable first, clinical underneath

Health data is only useful if it reads at a glance without being misread. I
designed gauge-style heroes for sleep, mood, heart, and blood sugar, and
translated American Heart Association guidance into colored zone banding for
blood pressure, so a reading lands inside a clinically meaningful range rather
than beside an abstract number. The same rule runs through accessibility. Every
chart exposes a plain-language summary, and my WCAG 2.2 patterns document
records each pattern twice, as built and as its production equivalent, so
engineering inherits decisions.

## Results

The prototype is the team's design source of truth, the place the team
validates real behavior before committing production work. The meal photo scan
shipped into the main product surface. And the practice itself may be the most
durable outcome, a workflow of worktrees, same-day pull requests, and promotion
gates that lets design move at the speed of conversation while holding a
quality line the whole team can see.
