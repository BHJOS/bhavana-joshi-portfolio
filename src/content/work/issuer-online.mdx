## Challenge

When I joined, the effort to revamp a decade-old portal was already underway. Having worked
in healthcare design for the past nine years, fintech design was a completely new domain
for me. To familiarise myself with the product, I conducted a comprehensive tool audit,
tested various flows, and reviewed existing user feedback. Through discovery and various
conversations with the design and product teams, I identified recurring issues that
hindered product development.

Key challenges:

- Design and development inconsistencies due to the absence of robust design components.
- Difficulty in adding new features because of inconsistent design patterns and technical
  debt.
- Transitioning the team from UXPin to Figma within a few months.

## My Role

As lead designer, my responsibilities included:

**Research.** Delving into the latest user research and data analysis to guide and support
design decisions. Understanding user behaviour and priorities to reshape navigation and
redesign an experience that empowered users.

**Design.** Collaborating with the product owner and business analysts to grasp business
needs. Working closely with in-house design and development teams to brainstorm and refine
designs that align user needs with business goals. Leading cost-saving initiatives by
shifting from UXPin to Figma, creating a comprehensive style library, templates, and
reusable components.

I worked alongside the Head of UX, a Product Owner, Business Analysts, UX designers, and
development teams.

## Process

### User persona

Of the five shortlisted personas, the Computershare Relationship Manager (RM) was
identified as the power user. Based on user feedback we discovered that clients were more
inclined to call the RMs rather than log into the portal. The goal to empower the
Relationship Manager was a top priority.

<Figure src={persona} altTodo width={600} caption="Sarah Gomez, 34 — Relationship Manager (power user)" />

Sarah's responsibilities are to build strong trusting relationships with clients, provide
personalised solutions to meet diverse client needs, and ensure high client satisfaction
and retention. Her challenges are juggling the varied and specific needs of different
clients, staying informed about each client's changing business environment, and providing
consistent high-quality service across different client segments.

### Navigation and templates

Based on Google Analytics and the frequency of web pages visited, there was a need to
revisit the site map because, in the ongoing design, some of the key pages were buried
within the sub-menu. The existing framework for the template posed a significant barrier to
adding contextual tools and resources and quick links to documents and relevant sections,
thereby limiting the site's functionality.

### Style guide and components

After multiple iterations and feedback from the key stakeholders, I started to build a
library of components and templates based on immediate needs and priority. The approved
batch was added to the Figma library, providing a comprehensive resource for designing new
prototypes to enhance the overall product offering.

Filters, tables and date pickers were among the most extensively used features in the
reporting section. Building a set of reusable components expedited the process of
prototyping one of the largest sections in the portal.

## Key Decisions

**Letting analytics reorder the navigation, not opinion.** Google Analytics showed that
key pages were buried in the sub-menu while low-traffic pages sat at the top level.
Reorganising against real page-visit frequency — rather than the existing internal
hierarchy — was what made the rest of the redesign possible.

**Designing for the Relationship Manager first.** Five personas were shortlisted, but the
research showed clients were phoning RMs rather than logging in. Treating the RM as the
power user, and designing to empower them, addressed the call-centre load at its source.

**Building the component library by priority, not completeness.** Rather than attempting a
full design system up front, I built components against immediate needs — filters, tables,
date pickers first, since they dominated the reporting section. That got the largest
section prototyped fastest.

## Outcome

Our combined efforts culminated in a compelling product that not only boasted rich features
but also provided clear, intuitive navigation, significantly enhancing the user experience.

- Redesigned a product that significantly enhanced the user experience with more intuitive
  navigation and added features, empowering users in their daily communications and
  interactions with clients.
- Introduced a more user-friendly self-service option that encouraged clients to access
  their individual accounts for quicker responses, significantly reducing the workload of
  the call centre and enhancing overall customer satisfaction.
- Successfully transitioned the team from UXPin to Figma, building a comprehensive style
  library, components, and templates.

### Future enhancements

Based on the most recent feedback, further iterations and enhancements to the design are
underway. The upcoming launch will showcase a product that offers an interactive dashboard
with real-time analytics and customisable widgets based on user needs, conformance to WCAG
2.2 guidelines, light and dark themes, and intuitive navigation with new features.
