## Clients weren't logging in — they were calling.

User feedback on the decade-old Issuer Services Portal revealed that clients preferred
phoning their Computershare Relationship Manager over using the portal. Rather than fight
that behaviour, we designed for it: empower the RM as the portal's power user, and make
self-service so clear that routine questions stopped needing a phone call at all. That
decision shaped everything that follows.

## Context and my role

As Senior UX Designer on the Issuer Services Portal — the registry management and insights
product of Computershare's largest business unit — I led the ongoing redesign effort:
research synthesis, information architecture, the component library, and the design org's
migration from UXPin to Figma. Team: Head of UX, Product Owner, Business Analysts, UX
designers, and development teams. Fintech was a new domain for me; I ramped by auditing the
tool, testing its flows end to end, and mining existing user feedback before proposing
anything.

Key challenges:

- Design and development inconsistencies due to the absence of robust design components.
- Difficulty in adding new features because of inconsistent design patterns and technical
  debt.
- Transitioning the team from UXPin to Figma within a few months.

## Research: the Relationship Manager

Five personas were shortlisted; analytics and feedback pointed to one as the power user:
the Relationship Manager. Sarah, an RM, juggles the specific needs of many issuer clients
and is judged on satisfaction and retention. Every design decision was tested against her
question: does this help me answer a client faster than a phone call?

## Rebuilding the navigation

Google Analytics showed key pages buried in sub-menus while high-traffic destinations sat
two taps deep. I reorganised the sitemap around actual visit frequency, validated the new
structure with the design and development teams for feasibility, and rebuilt the page
templates so contextual tools, quick links, and documents could live beside the content
they support.

## The design system and the Figma migration

I led the design org's migration from UXPin to Figma, building the style library,
templates, and reusable components as we went — so the migration paid for itself in
prototyping speed. Filters, tables, and date pickers — the workhorses of the reporting
section, the portal's largest area — became a reusable kit that cut prototype assembly for
new features from days to hours.

## Results and what shipped next

The redesigned portal shipped with clearer navigation, a component-based design language,
and a self-service model that reduced call-center dependency — clients found answers in
the portal instead of on the phone. The system carried forward: the WCAG 2.2 conformance
pass, dark theme, and real-time analytics dashboard entered development on the foundations
this work established.
