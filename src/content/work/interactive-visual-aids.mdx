Pharmaceutical sales representatives use Interactive Visual Aids (IVAs) to communicate with
healthcare professionals. These presentations engage physicians about the efficacy, dosing,
mechanism of action, and safety data of drug therapies approved by the FDA. They are
designed and hosted on a proprietary Veeva platform and delivered by the sales rep through
iPad Pro.

<PullQuote>
  Veeva controls the database and content presentation, Apple iOS controls interactivity,
  and BMS medical-legal teams and the FDA regulate the content and messaging. The challenge
  is to bring it all together seamlessly through design and delivery.
</PullQuote>

## Challenge

- Lengthy lead times from concept to layout design.
- Lengthy lead times for production.
- High cost.
- Bespoke and inconsistent design approach across brands, and also within the same brand.
- Unintuitive navigation.
- Sales reps requiring long training sessions before launch.

### Success criteria

Raise efficiencies. Lower design cost. Lower production cost. Speedy delivery. Consistent
branding. Consistent user experience. Scalable. Reduced training for sales reps.

## My Role

UX, UI, QA Testing, Storyboarding.

My design process involves keeping the user experience front and centre. I draw insights
from the brief and data collected during the initial research. Identifying users' pain
points is key to providing an optimised solution. Rounds of iterations based on feedback
received from different stakeholders, and users in test environments and post-launch, help
fine-tune a product in its multiple phases.

## Process

### User persona

<Figure src={userProfile} altTodo width={600} caption="Samantha — the pharmaceutical sales rep" />

Samantha's responsibilities are learning about her assigned therapeutic area and accessing
customer history to create more relevant, compliant messaging for healthcare professionals.
Her challenge is that she gets less than three minutes with the healthcare professional to
communicate all the details about the product. She needs quick access to all the approved
pieces of content, and must be able to switch easily between content to address any
questions the HCP may have about a drug's safety, efficacy, dosing or clinical study.

### Ideation

Keeping scalability in mind, we standardised the template designs that would work across
all brands, and a UI kit that nailed down all the specifics for the design and development
process. Creating and developing a global template and a global UI kit helped meet the
target and strategic objectives. This effort was a collaboration with the client, the brand
Agency of Record, and the Tag team.

under the umbrella brand of Bristol Myers Squibb.

### User testing

Multiple rounds of user testing by the in-house QA team, designers, and other stakeholders
erased the possibility of bugs and user errors.

## Key Decisions

**Standardising the template before styling any brand.** Building one global framework and
UI kit first — then applying brand styles on top — is what made the savings replicable. A
brand-by-brand approach would have reproduced the original inconsistency.

**Designing around a three-minute conversation.** Samantha gets under three minutes with a
physician. That constraint drove the thumbnail jump-navigation, the collapsible safety
tray, and the global reference slide — all of which exist to remove taps.

**Making forced flow a design feature, not a compliance afterthought.** Regulatory
requirements around Important Safety Information were built into the swipe interaction
itself, so compliance did not depend on the rep remembering to present it.
